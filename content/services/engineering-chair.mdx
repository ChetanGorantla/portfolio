## Summary

I'm Engineering Chair at Texas Convergent, where I lead the curriculum for a 100+ member engineering org. That means 5 workshops every semester on system design and cloud architecture. I also launched the org's secure member portal, which has served 700+ applicants, and shut down check-in fraud with rotating QR codes.

## Context

## Approach

> The curriculum is 5 workshops a semester, built around two subjects: system design and cloud architecture. The runbook index on the dashboard lists them.
> To teach a concept, I describe the motivation and high level design, and then dive deeper into design decisions and tradeoffs behind the tool or system. Then, I demonstrate examples in the real-world that it appears in, and ask members to brainstorm other use cases.

## The hard part

**Teaching peers.**

> Being confident is one thing, but being knowlegdeable enough to back up my presentations when questions are asked is another thing. To do so, I do a lot of research on these topics and digest as much intuition as I can.

**Still building.** Two pieces of the org's infrastructure are mine:

- **Member portal.** A secure internal portal on Next.js, Supabase, and PostgreSQL, serving 700+ applicants.
- **QR check-in.** A check-in code that never changes can be screenshotted and replayed by someone who isn't in the room. I built rotating-token QR authentication that refreshes every second, which eliminated replay-attack check-in fraud.

## Resolution

- 5 workshops per semester for a 100+ member engineering org.
- Member portal live, serving 700+ applicants.
- Replay-attack check-in fraud eliminated with QR tokens that rotate every second.

## Lessons learned

> Teaching someone else is the best way to learn. I've grown my intuition as an engineer by studying systems, and I've improved my communication and leadership ability by taking ownership of these students' curriculum.

## If I did it again

> I would try to step deeper into the shoes of the members and learn what exactly they seek to get out of my curriculum, so that I can tailor the topics towards their interests, treating it as a feedback loop.
