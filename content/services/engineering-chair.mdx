## In short

I lead the curriculum for Texas Convergent's 100+ member engineering org: 5 workshops a semester on system design and cloud architecture. I also launched the org's secure member portal (700+ applicants) and ended attendance fraud with rotating QR codes.

## The problem

Members have room to build projects at Convergent, but little exposure to what makes them scale. My job is to show them the tools used across the industry, and how distributed systems are built to scale.

## What I built

- **The curriculum:** each concept goes from motivation and high-level design, to the decisions and tradeoffs behind it, to real-world examples, then members brainstorm their own use cases
- **Member portal:** Next.js, Supabase, and PostgreSQL, serving 700+ applicants
- **QR check-in:** tokens that rotate every second, which ended replay-attack check-in fraud

## The hard part

**Teaching peers.** Being confident is one thing; knowing enough to back up a presentation when the questions start is another. To get there, I research each topic thoroughly and build as much intuition as I can.

## What I learned

Teaching is the best way to learn. Studying systems to teach them has sharpened my engineering intuition, and owning the curriculum has improved my communication and leadership. Next, I want to treat it as a feedback loop and tailor topics to what members want from it.
