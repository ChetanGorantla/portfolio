## In short

DetectFX listens to a guitar recording, identifies which of 13 effects are on it, and recreates the tone with DSP effects I wrote in NumPy and SciPy.

## The problem

I'm learning guitar, which mostly means hearing a tone and having no idea how to get it. Tube Screamer or RAT? Spring reverb or plate? Answering by ear usually means buying pedals until something sounds right, and online software is expensive.

## What I built

- **Classify:** a 13-class effect classifier trained on the EGFxSet dataset
- **Measure:** an 18-dimensional audio feature delta from FFT analysis
- **Recreate:** calibrated rules map that delta to 9 conditional effects, drawn from 10 DSP effects I built: filters, feedback delay networks, and waveshaping

## The hard part

**512 MB.** Inference had to fit in a 512 MB Heroku dyno. I used staged profiling to find where the memory went, one stage at a time. One early find: the 47 MB classifier was being loaded from disk on every request.

## What I learned

**Measure first, then fix.** My deploy-day commits were mostly guesses until I logged memory after every stage. Next time I'd index features by name: the rules read the feature vector by position, and the rule labeled "rms" actually reads spectral rolloff.
