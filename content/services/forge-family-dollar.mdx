## Summary

**Status: in progress.** This is a live incident update, not a postmortem. The work is still running.

> Texas Convergent's Forge program has a partnership with Family Dollar that spans the whole company. I'm the Tech Lead for it: I lead the engineering initiative for 2 projects under the partnership, and I work directly with the CIO.

## Context

> Family Dollar faces an issue of out-of-stock shelves generating poor trends in store efficiency and sell rate. Also, pinpad skimmers maliciously target EBT cardholders since their services offer less customer protection than credit card companies do.

## Approach

> For out-of-stock shelves, we identify high-risk shelves and derive fullness scores, and observe trends over time. For pinpad skimmers, we use two-pass object detection to first locate a pinpad and then identify certain characteristics. We compare computer-generated results with hand-labeled data for accuracy purposes.

## The hard part

> Working in a real-world environment brings tighter constraints than working on a personal project. Navigating cost, security, and feasibility is the most difficult aspect of the project - not the code. Finding a balance between the three requires communication across company stakeholders and teams to find an aligned perspective and acknowledge certain tradeoffs.

## Resolution

Not resolved yet, and that's expected. This report will be updated as the projects ship into production at Family Dollar.

## Lessons learned (so far)

> Leading a team is harder than it sounds. I've had to defend my teams' decisions, weigh outcomes on the fly, and maintain a great personal understanding of what's going on at every step of development. On top of that, keeping morale high when we face challenges is difficult, but necessary. As a leader, I'm prepared for any challenges we may face.
