## Summary

Ontology won **Best Use of Gemini** at a Google hackathon, out of 200+ teams. It's a Next.js/TypeScript platform that ingests PDFs, GitHub, and Slack into a typed knowledge graph, then answers questions over it with a Gemini 2.5 tool-calling RAG loop that validates its citations.

## Approach

- **Ingestion.** PDFs, GitHub, and Slack go in through an async job queue and come out as a typed, 250-node knowledge graph.
- **Retrieval.** A Gemini 2.5 tool-calling RAG loop pulls the top 7 matches from pgvector, with context caching so the same context isn't sent twice.
- **Isolation.** Tenants are kept apart with Postgres row-level security.

## The hard part

**The same thing, named three ways.** A person or project shows up in a PDF, a repo, and a Slack thread, written slightly differently each time. I deduplicated entities with cosine-similarity embeddings, then used Union-Find to merge matches. That way, near-duplicates connected only through a chain of matches still end up as one node.

**Answers you can check.** Every answer goes through citation validation, so its claims point back to real sources instead of plausible-sounding ones.

## Resolution

- Won Best Use of Gemini, out of 200+ teams.
- A typed 250-node knowledge graph built from PDFs, GitHub, and Slack.
- Top-7 pgvector retrieval with context caching and citation validation. Tenants isolated by row-level security.
