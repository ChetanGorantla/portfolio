## In short

Ontology won **Best Use of Google Gemini** at a UT Austin hackathon, out of 200+ teams. It ingests PDFs, GitHub, and Slack into a typed knowledge graph, then answers questions with a Gemini 2.5 tool-calling RAG loop that validates its citations.

## The problem

A team's knowledge ends up split across PDFs, a repo, and Slack, and the usual chat box on top answers confidently without showing its work. We wanted every claim to trace back to a source.

## What we built

- **Ingestion:** an async job queue turns PDFs, GitHub, and Slack into a typed, 250-node knowledge graph
- **Retrieval:** top-7 pgvector matches with context caching, so the same context isn't sent twice
- **Isolation:** tenants kept apart with Postgres row-level security

## The hard part

**The same thing, named three ways.** People and projects show up across PDFs, repos, and Slack under slightly different names. I deduplicated entities with cosine-similarity embeddings, then merged matches with Union-Find, so near-duplicates linked only through a chain still become one node.

## What I learned

**Agree on the seams first.** We wrote specs and a schema doc before any code, and stubbed the AI functions with fixed signatures, so frontend, backend, and AI work ran in parallel. Next time I'd also check that a quoted citation actually appears in its chunk, not just that the chunk exists.
