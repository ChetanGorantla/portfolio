## In short

An evacuation simulator from an NVIDIA hackathon. LLaMA 3.1 plays the individual occupants, making 6 routing decisions per tick at 17K tokens per second, while a deterministic engine keeps the physics honest. Monte Carlo trials run across Kafka workers to find bottlenecks.

## The problem

Most evacuation math treats people like fluid. Real occupants know only some of the exits, follow the crowd, freeze, or turn back, and those small choices add up to a jammed stairwell next to an empty exit.

## What I built

- **Decisions:** LLaMA 3.1 picks routes from options the engine offers; the engine owns movement, queueing, timing, and behavior archetypes.
- **Routing:** a weighted graph search ranks the k shortest routes per exit by hazard, congestion, and crowd popularity
- **Scale:** Monte Carlo trials across Kafka consumer-group workers, added after the hackathon along with hazards, multi-floor buildings, and IFC import (a teammate built the visual building editor)

## The hard part

**LLMs will route people through walls.** Graph-grounded guardrails check every proposed route and reject hallucinated or discontinuous ones. And 6 decisions per tick only works if inference keeps up, so GPU throughput had to go from 1× to 100×.

## What I learned

**Plan for the model not showing up.** Calls time out and small models return malformed JSON, so every decision has a deterministic fallback, and every prompt-only trait has a mechanical mirror in it. Next time I'd build the LLM-off baseline first: it's the one comparison the project exists for.
